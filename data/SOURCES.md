# Sources — how blind students learn (pedagogy layer)

These are the sources behind the teaching rules injected into every model call
(`js/pedagogy.js`). Verify titles/quotes before putting any on a slide.

## Core pedagogy

1. **Lowenfeld, B. (1973). *The Visually Handicapped Child in School.* New York: John Day.**
   The classic "three principles" of teaching blind children:
   **concreteness** (real, touchable referents), **learning by doing**, and
   **unified experiences** (blind students receive fragments; instruction must
   explicitly connect parts into wholes). → rules 1, 3, 7.

2. **Fraiberg, S. (1977). *Insights from the Blind: Comparative Studies of Blind and Sighted Infants.* Basic Books.**
   Foundational observational research on how congenitally blind children build
   concepts of objects and space without vision. → rule 5.

3. **Millar, S. (1994). *Understanding and Representing Space: Theory and Evidence from Studies with Blind and Sighted Children.* Oxford University Press.**
   Touch is a **sequential** sense: spatial understanding is assembled
   part-by-part rather than grasped at a glance. → rule 2 (part-to-whole).

4. **Braille Authority of North America (2010). *Guidelines and Standards for Tactile Graphics.***
   Practice standard for converting visual graphics: simplify, segment,
   present sequentially, name parts before relations. → rules 2, 4.

## Congenital vs late blindness (neuroscience)

5. **Bedny, M., Pascual-Leone, A., Dodell-Feder, D., Fedorenko, E., & Saxe, R. (2011). "Language processing in the occipital cortex of congenitally blind adults." *PNAS* 108(11).**
   The "visual" cortex of people blind from birth is recruited for language —
   their conceptual system is intact but built on non-visual input. → rule 5.

6. **Kim, J. S., Elli, G. V., & Bedny, M. (2019). "Knowledge of animal appearance among sighted and blind adults." *PNAS* 116(23).**
   Congenitally blind adults hold appearance knowledge (color, look) as
   **inferred, told information**, not perceptual experience. Color words may
   label, but cannot carry, meaning. → rule 5a.

7. **Hollins, M. (1989). *Understanding Blindness: An Integrative Approach.* Lawrence Erlbaum.**
   Late-blind individuals retain usable visual imagery; congenitally blind do
   not. Different description strategies are required. → rules 5a vs 5b.

## Practice conventions

8. **Orientation & Mobility (O&M) professional practice** (e.g., TSBVI curricula;
   RNIB teaching guidance): clock-face directions and body-anchored spatial
   language are the standard way to communicate layout without sight. → rule 4.

## Honest caveats (say these if asked)

- Rules 6 (audio working-memory budget) and 7 (frequent checks) are standard
  practice in audio-first instruction and general working-memory research,
  not tied to a single citation.
- No blind student has tested this build yet. The pedagogy layer encodes the
  literature; user validation is the committed next step.
